The setup — ~20 minutes, once

Step 1 — Grayscale (about 5 minutes) (randomized field experiment: design friction cut objectively measured screen time immediately, beating self-committed time limits)

  • [ ] Turn on grayscale: iPhone — Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size → Color Filters → Grayscale. Android — search Settings for "grayscale" or "color correction."
  • [ ] Build the escape hatch: iPhone — Accessibility Shortcut → Color Filters (triple-click toggles color). Android — add the grayscale quick-settings tile.
  • [ ] Rule of use: toggling color for maps, photos, or a chart is a decision, not a defeat. What to expect: the feed looks like homework. That's the mechanism, not a bug.

Step 2 — Batch notifications to three windows (about 10 minutes) (randomized field experiment, n = 237: three daily batches → more attentive, better mood, more control, lower stress, fewer interruptions; notifications OFF entirely → higher anxiety and FoMO)

  • [ ] Pick three windows anchored to your routine (e.g., 8:00, 12:30, 6:00).
  • [ ] iPhone — Settings → Notifications → Scheduled Summary → your three times → add your five noisiest apps. Android — scheduled Do Not Disturb between windows, and/or set noisy apps' notifications to silent delivery.
  • [ ] Keep calls, real texts, and calendar alerts live. Do NOT turn everything off — that's the arm that backfired. What to expect: a day or two of phantom-check urges, then the checking clusters around your windows.

Step 3 — The 10-minute feed audit (two randomized experiments run during the pandemic, N = 299 and N = 603: 2–4 minutes of negative news measurably dropped mood — kindness content didn't)

  • [ ] Open your most-scrolled app. Mute or unfollow the last five accounts that reliably make you feel worse.
  • [ ] Follow two accounts you'd want in front of you at 11 p.m.
  • [ ] News lives in your batch windows now, not in the ambient drip. What to expect: the feed feels oddly quiet for a few days. Let it.

The grade, two weeks out: where did the evening hours go? (The same grayscale trial found no immediate well-being gain from cutting screen time — so grade this in hours reclaimed, not mood.)

The two maintenance rules

The deliberate-exception rule. Color back on for a vacation's photos? Notifications live for the day you're expecting the call? Fine — that's a decision, made in advance, with an end point. A decision is not a relapse. The setup serves your life, not the reverse.

The relapse rule. Some night next month you may look up and find yourself full-color, pings on, three hours gone. Here is the entire required response: re-run the 20-minute setup. That's it. No inquest into your character, no streak reset, no penance week. You already know the guilt ritual doesn't work — you counted its results in section one.

This rule is the quiet reason the setup beats willpower at its own game. Willpower has to win every single night, forever, and one loss starts the shame spiral that ends in the 1:40 a.m. scroll. The setup only has to be re-run — once, in 20 minutes, any evening you notice it's eroded. Cheap to redo is the strongest thing a defense can be.
